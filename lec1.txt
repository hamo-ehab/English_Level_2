                            Vocabulary
Acquaintances: People you know but are not very close to.
Example: I have a few acquaintances at the gym, but we don’t talk outside of
workouts.

Neighbours: People who live next to or near you.
Example: Our neighbours often invite us over for a barbecue.

Manager: The person in charge of employees at a workplace.
Example: My manager gave me a new project to work on this week.

Lecturer: A teacher at a university or college.
Example: Our lecturer explained the concept very clearly during today’s class.
                           Vocabulary
Tutors: Instructors who help students with learning, often in small groups or one-on-one.
Example: My tutor helped me prepare for the math exam.


Classmates: People in the same class or course.
Example: My classmates and I worked together on the science project.


Roommates: People who share the same room or apartment with you.
Example: My roommate and I split the rent each month.


Housemates: People who live in the same house or apartment as you.
Example: I share an apartment with two housemates.
                           Vocabulary
Colleagues: People who work with you at a job.
Example: My colleagues and I often have lunch together after meetings.


Employer: A person or company that hires people to work for them.
Example: My employer gave me two weeks of paid vacation this year.


Co-workers: People who work with you in the same company or office.
Example: My co-worker helped me with the project yesterday.

              Phrasal verbs
Phrasal verbs are combinations of a verb + preposition that create a new
    meaning, often different from the individual meanings of the words.
    Mastering these can be tricky since the meaning isn’t always literal.
   Below are common phrasal verbs with their definitions and examples.

Take after (someone)
  Meaning: To resemble a family member in
  appearance, personality, or behavior.
  Example:
  Sarah takes after her father; they both have the
  same sense of humor.
  Do you take after your mother or father more?
Look after (someone/something)


     Meaning: To take care of someone or
      something.
      Example:
      Can you look after my cat while I’m away?
      He looks after his younger siblings when his
      parents are busy.
Get on (with someone)

                    Meaning: To have a good relationship with
                     someone; to get along.
                     Example:
                     I get on really well with my neighbors.
                     Do you get on with your coworkers?
Bring up (someone)

    Meaning: To raise or care for children,
     teaching them life skills and values.
     Example:
     She was brought up by her grandparents.
     It’s not easy to bring up children in today’s
     world.
Fall out (with someone)
   Meaning: To have an argument or
    disagreement that damages the
    relationship.
    Example:
    I fell out with my best friend over a
    misunderstanding.
    Have you ever fallen out with a family
    member?
Look up to (someone )
   Meaning: To admire or respect someone, usually because of their
    achievements or qualities.
    Example:
    I look up to my older brother for his dedication and hard work.
    Who do you look up to the most in your life?
